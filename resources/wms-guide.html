ABC analysis

ABC analysis groups items by a chosen measure of importance, such as activity or value, so review effort can be prioritized.

How it fits

The classes and thresholds depend on the policy and the measure used. A fast-moving item classification is not automatically the same as a high-value classification.

Example

A warehouse may choose to count its highest-priority item class more frequently.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Advance shipping notices

An ASN describes a shipment expected to arrive. It is sent before receipt and can include expected items and quantities.

How it fits

An ASN is different from a PO: the PO describes the purchase request, while the ASN describes an upcoming shipment. Actual receipt establishes what arrived.

Example

A PO requests 100 units; an ASN announces the first delivery of 60.

AI-assisted development

TK WMS was built using AI as part of its development process. Plain-language needs and feedback help shape implementation work that can be reviewed and refined.

How it fits

Building a product with AI does not imply that every warehouse decision uses an AI model. Ella’s current website replies use saved knowledge and local matching without an AI service.

Example

A request to clarify a workflow can become a reviewable change to code or product documentation.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Allocation & reservation

Allocation or reservation commits eligible stock to demand. It is different from physically collecting that stock during picking.

How it fits

Status, attributes, and configured eligibility rules affect stock selection. Reservation Attribute Profiles in TK WMS define attributes used by its reservation controls.

Example

An order can have stock allocated before a picker collects it.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Backorders & shortages

A backorder represents demand that remains unfulfilled rather than being fully shipped immediately. A stockout means the required stock is unavailable for the relevant need.

How it fits

Recorded on-hand stock may still be ineligible or already allocated. Review the demand, quantity, status, and stock-selection context before assuming a physical shortage.

Example

An order can remain partly unfulfilled while the operation waits for eligible stock.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Barcode identification

A barcode represents data in a machine-readable form. Scanning reads the encoded value; the application decides how that value is interpreted.

How it fits

An item code, handling-unit identifier, and location code represent different entities. A successful scan does not by itself confirm that a business transaction completed.

Example

Scanning a location identifies the location; confirmation of a move is a separate workflow event.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Batch picking

Batch picking collects items for multiple orders in a combined picking effort, followed by sorting or assignment to the correct orders.

How it fits

It differs from picking each order independently. The workflow must preserve the relationship between the collected units and the orders they fulfill.

Example

A picker collects a shared item for several orders in one trip.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Bills of lading & packing lists

A bill of lading is a transport document associated with the movement of goods. A packing list describes shipment contents.

How it fits

These documents have different purposes and are not substitutes for a PO, ASN, or actual receipt. Required contents and handling depend on the transport and business context.

Example

A shipment can have a contents list and separate transport documentation.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Business rules & workflow policies

TK WMS includes Business Rules Assistant setup, Rule Actions, and Workflow Policies for authorized configuration work.

How it fits

Rule setup addresses configured behavior; actions address its operational outcome; workflow policies concern supported execution policy. Screen design is a separate concern.

Example

A policy question belongs in configuration review, while an overdue task belongs in operational follow-up.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Cartonization

Cartonization concerns selecting or planning suitable shipping containers for goods. It can use configured item and container information.

How it fits

It differs from the physical act of packing. The data and rules used depend on the implementation; a carton definition alone does not imply automatic optimal box selection.

Example

A shipment may need its items divided among appropriate cartons.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Cluster picking

Cluster picking handles several orders on one route while keeping their goods separated into identified containers or positions.

How it fits

It differs from collecting a shared batch and sorting later. Container identification and confirmations are important to keeping orders separate.

Example

A cart has a separate tote position for each order on the route.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Cross-docking

Cross-docking coordinates eligible incoming goods with outgoing demand so goods can move through the facility without the usual storage path.

How it fits

It differs from ordinary putaway into storage. Eligibility and the inbound-outbound relationship need to be reviewed under the configured process.

Example

Eligible received goods are coordinated with an outbound requirement.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Cycle counting

Cycle counting verifies selected inventory or locations rather than counting the entire facility at once. Count plans organize scope, tasks track work, and variance review handles differences.

How it fits

A general review sequence is choose scope, perform the count, investigate differences, and resolve them through an authorized process. A count is not automatically an adjustment.

Example

Count a defined set of locations during a shift and review any differences.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Dashboards & KPIs

Dashboards summarize warehouse work or performance by function. A KPI is a defined measure used to review a particular aspect of performance.

How it fits

TK WMS names dashboards for Putaway, Picking, Allocation, Replenishment, Inbound Performance, Task Activity, Consolidation, and Picking Performance. Metric definitions and time windows must be understood before comparing values.

Example

A functional view highlights an area needing attention; a query investigates the individual record.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Dock appointments & yard activity

A dock appointment represents planned arrival or service time. Yard activity concerns the movement or status of vehicles and trailers around the facility.

How it fits

Arrival planning and receipt processing are distinct: a vehicle may have arrived without its goods having been fully received.

Example

An expected truck has an appointment; the receiving team separately processes the delivery.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
FEFO

FEFO means First Expired, First Out: prioritize eligible stock with the earliest expiry date.

How it fits

The oldest receipt is not necessarily the earliest-expiring batch. FEFO needs reliable expiry information and rules about stock eligibility and customer requirements.

Example

A newer arrival expiring next month can be selected before an older arrival expiring next year.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

FIFO

FIFO means First In, First Out: select older received stock before newer stock, subject to eligibility rules.

How it fits

FIFO is based on the relevant arrival or receipt sequence. It differs from FEFO, which prioritizes expiry dates, and is a stock-selection concept rather than proof of a particular TK WMS setting.

Example

Eligible stock received on Monday is considered before comparable stock received on Friday.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Full physical inventory

A full physical inventory verifies stock across the defined facility or inventory scope in a coordinated count exercise.

How it fits

It differs from cycle counting, which checks smaller portions repeatedly. Movement controls, cutoffs, and reconciliation procedures help keep a count interpretable.

Example

A scheduled count covers all locations in a defined warehouse area.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Implementation & training

WMS implementation combines process review, master-data preparation, configuration, validation, user training, and deployment planning.

How it fits

UAT means User Acceptance Testing: checking the implemented workflows against intended user needs. TK WMS exposes Training Phases 1–6, but their exact curriculum is not documented here.

Example

Validate a representative receipt-to-shipment workflow and exception paths before relying on a new setup.

Inbound inspections

Inspection checks whether received goods meet the required condition or acceptance criteria before the next warehouse step.

How it fits

A damaged package or quantity issue may require separate review. The approval process and whether goods remain held depend on the configured procedure.

Example

A receiver identifies damaged cartons and routes them for review.

Integration, API & EDI

An API is an interface applications use to exchange requests and responses. EDI exchanges structured business documents between systems or organizations.

How it fits

TK WMS documents Interface Designer, Integration Sync, import/export, and an API playground. The guide does not confirm a named third-party connector, EDI message type, authentication method, or automatic synchronization behavior.

Example

An integration design needs clear identifiers, document ownership, timing, and error handling.

Inventory accuracy

Inventory accuracy describes agreement between recorded stock and verified physical stock under a defined measure.

How it fits

Specify whether the measure concerns units, locations, lines, or another scope. Counting, discrepancy investigation, and authorized corrections contribute to control; no accuracy percentage is promised here.

Example

A unit-level accuracy measure is not interchangeable with a location-level measure.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Inventory adjustments

An inventory adjustment changes recorded inventory to correct a discrepancy or record an authorized stock event.

How it fits

A move changes location; an adjustment changes an inventory record. Count findings and supporting evidence should be reviewed under the warehouse’s procedure before correction.

Example

A verified discrepancy may lead to an authorized adjustment.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Inventory control

Inventory control tracks what stock is recorded, where it is, and its status. A workbench can help review on-hand and allocated quantities by item and location.

How it fits

Looking up stock does not change it. Adjustments, moves, holds, and releases are separate authorized actions with different purposes.

Example

Review a stock record before deciding whether it needs a move or a quantity correction.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Inventory holds

A hold identifies stock requiring restricted handling or follow-up. Releasing a hold is a separate action from moving or adjusting the stock.

How it fits

The exact effect on allocation and picking depends on configured status and eligibility rules. Review the reason and authorization before any release.

Example

Stock awaiting inspection may need to remain segregated or held.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
Kitting & bills of material

Kitting combines components into a kit. A bill of material defines the component structure, while assembly or disassembly work orders represent planned execution.

How it fits

TK WMS includes production stations and progress monitoring around this work. The kit definition is separate from an individual work order.

Example

A kit uses several component items according to its bill of material.

Related TK WMS guide ↗
LIFO

LIFO means Last In, First Out: the most recently placed or received eligible stock is handled first.

How it fits

Physical access patterns and configured selection rules matter. This warehouse explanation does not describe inventory accounting policy or confirm LIFO support in TK WMS.

Example

A physical stack accessible from one side may lead workers to handle the newest accessible unit first.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Location capacity & compatibility

Capacity concerns the space or load a location or container can handle. Compatibility concerns whether goods can appropriately share a location or handling context.

How it fits

Quantity, volume, weight, handling restrictions, and product data can affect these decisions. Which checks a WMS enforces must be verified in its configuration.

Example

An apparently empty location may still be unsuitable for a particular handling unit.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

Locations, aisles & zones

A warehouse location identifies a place where stock or work is represented. Aisles, zones, and layouts organize the physical or operational structure.

How it fits

The location definition is separate from the rules suggesting where goods should go. Pick routes also use warehouse context to organize travel paths.

Example

A location belongs within a defined warehouse area and may be part of a route.

Lots, batches & serial numbers

A lot or batch groups units with shared production or handling context. A serial number identifies an individual unit.

How it fits

Lot tracking answers which group a unit belongs to; serial tracking identifies a particular unit. The required identifiers and captured events depend on the product and setup.

Example

A batch can contain many units, each of which may also have its own serial number.

LPNs & handling units

An LPN, or license plate number, identifies a handling unit such as a pallet or carton. It helps associate the unit with its contents, location, and status.

How it fits

A handling-unit identifier is different from an item identifier. Moving a pallet’s LPN concerns the unit; a SKU identifies a type of item.

Example

One pallet LPN can represent the stock carried on that pallet.

Min/max replenishment

Min/max replenishment uses configured lower and target levels to identify when and how much stock should be replenished.

How it fits

Internal picking-location replenishment differs from purchasing new stock from a supplier. The trigger, quantities, and timing depend on configuration.

Example

A pick face below a configured threshold needs a refill toward its target level.

General WMS concept. TK WMS support is not confirmed by this definition.

On-hand, allocated & available stock

On-hand is the quantity physically recorded in inventory. Allocated stock has been committed to demand. Available stock is the quantity eligible for a purpose under the system’s rules.

How it fits

Available quantity is not always simply on-hand minus allocated: holds, status, and other eligibility rules can affect selection. Do not apply a formula without knowing the configured meaning.

Example

A warehouse records 100 units, with 30 allocated and some potentially held.

Order consolidation

Consolidation brings together goods or work from separate handling paths before the next fulfillment step. Shipment grouping coordinates shipment work under configured criteria.

How it fits

The correct grouping depends on order and shipping context. Combining work does not mean that unrelated orders or handling units can be mixed.

Example

Items picked in separate zones are brought together for packing.

Order picking

Picking collects the items needed for an order from warehouse locations. It follows the demand and stock-selection context established by the workflow.

How it fits

Picking is distinct from packing, which prepares collected goods for shipment. Confirmations and exception handling depend on the configured process.

Example

A picker collects the specified units before they are checked and packed.

Packing & verification

Packing prepares picked goods for shipment. Verification checks that the goods and quantities match the intended fulfillment work.

How it fits

Packing can include container selection, labels, and other configured checks. It is separate from selecting stock for demand or confirming carrier dispatch.

Example

Check the collected items before sealing their shipping carton.

Purchase orders

A purchase order is the buyer’s order to a supplier, describing goods and quantities requested. It establishes what was ordered, not what has already arrived.

How it fits

A PO can be delivered in several shipments. Compare the order, expected shipment notice, and actual receipt separately when reviewing inbound differences.

Example

An order for 100 units might arrive in deliveries of 60 and 40.

Putaway

Putaway moves received goods into storage locations. Directed putaway uses configured location suggestions; other workflows may let an authorized worker choose a location.

How it fits

Selection can depend on product characteristics, capacity, and configured rules. A location suggestion and the confirmed physical movement are different steps.

Example

Goods received at a dock are moved into an appropriate storage location.

Queries investigate individual records or history. TK WMS includes shipment, inventory, receipt, and transaction lookup paths plus serial and user-activity inquiries.

How it fits

These are distinct from dashboards, which summarize a function. An audit trail is only as complete as the events and identifiers actually recorded.

Use a shipment inquiry when investigating one shipment rather than relying only on an area-level dashboard.

Recall traceability

Recall work identifies affected goods and follows their movement or handling history. Traceability depends on the identifiers and events recorded.

How it fits

A recall investigation can involve lot, serial, inventory, and shipment information where those records are available. Avoid assuming any system has captured information it was never given.

Example

An affected batch prompts review of remaining stock and associated shipment history.

Receiving & receipts

Receiving records the goods that physically arrive at a warehouse. It involves identifying the delivery and checking actual items and quantities.

How it fits

A typical process reviews expected information, checks arrivals, records discrepancies, performs configured checks, and moves goods toward putaway. Receipt is distinct from an advance shipment notice.

Example

If 58 units arrive against an expected 60, the difference needs review.

Replenishment

Replenishment moves stock from reserve or another source location to a location needing stock, often a forward-pick face.

How it fits

A method defines the configured approach. A request describes work needed; a task represents work to assign and execute. Requests commonly include priority, status, and the movement required.

Example

Refill a picking location from reserve storage before its next planned work.

A warehouse label and a report can present related data in different formats.

Short picks & picking exceptions

A short pick occurs when the required quantity cannot be collected as expected. The cause may involve missing stock, status, location, or a recorded discrepancy.

How it fits

A general investigation reviews the requested item, location, handling unit, quantity, and stock status. Follow the warehouse’s authorized exception procedure rather than inventing a correction.

Slotting

Slotting plans where products should be stored or picked based on operational needs. It can consider handling, space, activity, and compatibility.

How it fits

Slotting is a planning concept; putaway executes a storage movement, while replenishment refills a location. The exact optimization capability varies by WMS.

Staging temporarily organizes goods before the next step, such as putaway, loading, or consolidation. Storage holds goods within the warehouse inventory structure.

How it fits

A staging location and a final storage location serve different operational purposes. The records and status associated with each depend on the configured workflow.
